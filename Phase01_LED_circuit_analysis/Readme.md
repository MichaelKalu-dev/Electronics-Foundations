# Phase 01 — LED Circuit Analysis

## Objective

Investigate the relationship between **Voltage**, **Current**, **Resistance**, **Voltage Drop**, and **Power** using a simple LED circuit through practical experimentation.

## Components Used

- 9V Battery
- LED
- 220Ω Resistor
- 470Ω Resistor
- Voltmeter
- Ammeter
- Connecting Wires

## Experiments Performed

1. Built a basic LED circuit using a 220Ω resistor.
2. Measured the battery voltage, LED voltage, resistor voltage, and circuit current.
3. Increased the resistor value from 220Ω to 470Ω.
4. Compared current, voltage drop, and LED brightness.
5. Removed the resistor completely to observe the effect of excessive current.

## Measurements

### Circuit with 220Ω Resistor

| Measurement | Value |
|-------------|-------|
| Battery Voltage | 9V |
| LED Voltage | 2.05V |
| Resistor Voltage | 6.95V |
| Current | 31.6mA |

### Circuit with 470Ω Resistor

| Measurement | Value |
|-------------|-------|
| LED Voltage | 1.97V |
| Resistor Voltage | 7.03V |
| Current | 15mA |

## Observations

- The sum of the LED voltage and resistor voltage equaled the battery voltage.
- Increasing the resistance reduced the circuit current.
- Increasing the resistance reduced the LED brightness.
- The LED voltage changed only slightly as the resistance increased.
- Removing the resistor caused excessive current and destroyed the LED in the simulation.

## Questions I Asked

### If current is not consumed, why does changing the load change the current?

The load determines how much current is allowed to flow. It does not consume the current.

### Does a resistor only affect current?

No. A resistor limits current and also produces a voltage drop when current flows through it.

### If voltage and current are not consumed, what is?

Electrical energy is transferred from the battery and converted into light, heat, sound, or motion depending on the component.

### Why do metals have resistance?

Moving electrons interact with vibrating atoms inside the material, creating opposition to the movement of charge.

### Why does an LED require a resistor?

The resistor limits the current to a safe value, preventing damage to the LED.
